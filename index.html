Sunday 10/4

Arrive Tokyo

On the road

Land at Haneda and get to the Minami-Senju area, where Rushcorp is based. The airport limousine bus stops outside the Asakusa View Hotel, which is one stop from Minami-Senju on the Tsukuba Express — the easiest door-to-door route with luggage.

Worth stopping for

  • Senso-ji and Nakamise-doriinfo — five minutes' walk from most Asakusa hotels, and best after dark when the day crowds have gone.
  • Kappabashi kitchenware streetinfo — a few hundred metres of chef's knives, cast iron and lacquerware, if you want something to ship home.
  • Hoppy-doriinfo — a lane of open-fronted izakaya behind Senso-ji, and the easiest first-night dinner in Asakusa.

Watch for

Sort your IDP, licence and passport into one pocket tonight. Rushcorp will want all three in the morning.

Monday 10/5

Tokyo → Shima Onsen

Tokyo190 kmShima Onsen
Open this day's route in Google Maps ↗

On the road

Pickup at Rushcorp, then north on the Kan-etsu to Shibukawa. From there R353 and R145 run west up the Agatsuma valley through Nakanojo, and a narrow road climbs the last few kilometres to Hinatami at the top of Shima Onsen, inside Joshin'etsu-kogen National Park. Deliberately easy for a first day — expressway for the dull part, one good valley at the end.

Worth stopping for

  • Agatsuma Gorgeinfo — a narrow rock canyon along R145, signposted from the road, with short walking paths if you want to stretch.
  • Yanba Daminfo — completed in 2020 after decades of controversy; the new lake and the viewing platforms are worth ten minutes.
  • Shima Blueinfo — Okushima Lake and Shima Lake hold an unusually vivid blue from suspended minerals. Best in flat morning light.
  • Hinatami Yakushidoinfo — a 1598 temple hall built over the spring, one of the oldest surviving onsen structures in Japan and an Important Cultural Property. A few minutes' walk from the inn.
  • Shima no Koseninfo — the little public bath beside the Yakushido, if you arrive with time to spare.

Watch for

Short ride, but you will be jet-lagged. Aim to be off the road by mid-afternoon.

Lodging

Shikanozoki-no-yu Tsuruya

Rated 4.1 from 280 reviews, at the quiet top end of the Shima valley. Four private baths plus rooms with their own tub — the large rock bath is what guests single out. English is spoken and reviewers repeatedly mention how well the staff handle foreign guests. A few note the price sits high for what the rooms are.

tsuruya.net ↗

Tuesday 10/6

Shima Onsen → Sanjo

Shima Onsen215 kmSanjo
Open this day's route in Google Maps ↗

On the road

Back down the valley to Nakanojo, north to Minakami, then R17 over Mikuni Pass into Niigata. This is the old highway and it emptied out when the expressway opened — long sweeping curves, and a genuine weather line at the top. Down through Yuzawa and across the Uonuma rice plain to Sanjo.

Worth stopping for

  • Mikuni Passinfo — the Gunma/Niigata border, and the point where the climate visibly changes.
  • Yuzawainfo — the snow country of Kawabata's novel, and a good coffee stop.
  • The Uonuma rice fieldsinfo — harvested and gold in October, and the source of the rice you will be eating all week.
  • Tsubame-Sanjoinfo — Japan's metalworking capital. Knife and tool workshops open to visitors, and the reason Snow Peak is headquartered here.

Watch for

The alternative is west through Kusatsu and over the Shiga-Kusatsu Kogen Route at Shibu Pass, 2,172 m, then down the Chikuma on R117 — about 260 km and the best road on the trip. It costs you roughly ninety minutes, and R292 has a seasonal night closure plus volcanic gas restrictions near Shirane.

Lodging

Snow Peak FIELD SUITE SPA HEADQUARTERS

Rated 4.4 from over 500 reviews — not a ryokan but a design resort on Snow Peak's own campfield. Architect-built suites and standalone cabins with decks, plus indoor and outdoor onsen and a sauna looking at the mountains. Reviewers rate the spa and the food highly and describe the site as exceptionally quiet at night. Tattoos are allowed in the baths only during limited hours, so confirm at check-in.

www.snowpeak.co.jp/fieldsuitespa/hq ↗

Wednesday 10/7

Sanjo → Kaminoyama

Sanjo230 kmKaminoyama
Open this day's route in Google Maps ↗

On the road

North across the Niigata plain to Shibata, then R113 east up the Arakawa valley — the old Yonezawa highway, a river road nearly the whole way — over the prefectural border at Oguni and through Nan'yo to Kaminoyama.

Worth stopping for

  • Michi-no-eki Sekikawa and the Arakawa gorgeinfo — the prettiest stretch of R113 and a natural lunch stop.
  • Oguniinfo — beech forest either side of the road, which should be turning at this altitude in early October.
  • Yonezawainfo — Uesugi Shrine and the castle grounds, and the obvious place to eat the beef at source. A short detour south.
  • Kaminoyama Castleinfo — and the samurai residences below it; free foot baths are dotted through the town.

Lodging

Nihon-no-yado Koyo

Rated 4.4 from over 2,200 reviews, which is a very large sample for a ryokan. Rooftop bath with an outdoor section, some rooms with private open-air tubs, and dinners built around Yonezawa beef and Tsuyahime rice. Breakfast is served in a large hall, which a few reviewers find impersonal for the price.

www.koyoga.com ↗

Thursday 10/8

Kaminoyama → Nikaho

Kaminoyama230 kmNikaho
Open this day's route in Google Maps ↗

On the road

North past Yamagata city, up R13 to Shinjo, then R47 west down the Mogami valley to Sakata and out to the coast. R7 runs north from there into Akita, finishing at Kisakata under the western flank of Mt. Chokai.

Worth stopping for

  • Yamadera (Risshaku-ji)info — a thousand stone steps up a cliff to a temple among the rocks, where Basho wrote the cicada haiku. Fifteen minutes off route; allow ninety.
  • Mogami River boat trips from Furukuchiinfo — flat-bottomed boats with a singing boatman.
  • Sakatainfo — the Sankyo rice warehouses with their row of zelkova trees, and the Homma Museum garden.
  • Kisakatainfo — Basho came to see a bay of ninety-nine islands; an 1804 earthquake lifted the seabed and the islands now sit in rice paddies. Odd and rather beautiful.

Watch for

Kisakata is known for rock oysters — worth asking the inn whether they are still in season.

Lodging

Ichie

Rated 3.7 from 250 reviews — the lowest-rated stop on the trip, and worth knowing before you arrive. The rooms were renovated a few years ago and reviewers praise the cypress open-air baths and the traditional breakfast; some rooms have a private sauna and cold plunge. The complaints are consistent too: an ageing building and inconsistent front-desk service.

nikaho-ichie.jp ↗

Friday 10/9

Nikaho → Shizukuishi

Nikaho220 kmShizukuishi
Open this day's route in Google Maps ↗

On the road

The Chokai Blue Line first thing — from near sea level to about 1,150 m at Hokodate in a series of tight switchbacks, with the Sea of Japan below you the whole way up. Back down, then inland through Yuzawa and Kakunodate, past Lake Tazawa and over Sengan Pass on R46 into Iwate.

Worth stopping for

  • Chokai Blue Line and the Hokodate viewpointinfo — go early; cloud builds on the mountain through the day.
  • Kakunodateinfo — the best-preserved samurai district in the north, dark wooden walls and weeping cherries. Twenty minutes' walk end to end.
  • Lake Tazawainfo — the deepest lake in Japan at 423 m, and a startling cobalt blue. The gilded Tatsuko statue stands in the shallows on the west shore.
  • Nyuto Onseninfo — up a side road from Tazawa; if you have a spare hour, Tsurunoyu's milky open-air bath is the famous one.

Watch for

The Chokai Blue Line is seasonal and closes for winter. Mid-October should be fine, but confirm before committing to it as your route.

Lodging

Hotel Mori-no-kaze Oshuku

Rated 4.2 from over 300 reviews, at Oshuku Onsen in Shizukuishi with Mt. Iwate behind it. Three linked buildings, large rooms, and options with a private in-room onsen. Reviewers say the exterior looks dated but the interiors are well kept, and single out the dining hall and the evening performance. Two minutes from the golf course.

www.morinokaze.com ↗

Saturday 10/10

Golf: Hachimantai Country Club

On the road

No riding. The course is effectively next door to the hotel — under two kilometres — so the panniers stay packed and the day is entirely yours.

Worth stopping for

  • Mt. Iwateinfo — fills the skyline from most of the course; mid-October should have the surrounding forest in full colour.
  • Hachimantai Aspite Lineinfo — an hour north, and a superb afternoon out-and-back if you finish early.
  • Koiwai Farminfo — twenty minutes east, for the tree-lined approach and the soft-serve.

Lodging

Hotel Mori-no-kaze Oshuku — second night

Same inn as last night — no packing, no check-in, and the baths are waiting when you come off the eighteenth.

www.morinokaze.com ↗

Sunday 10/11

Shizukuishi → Naruko

Shizukuishi200 kmNaruko
Open this day's route in Google Maps ↗

On the road

South the length of Iwate into Miyagi. If you want the Aspite Line, take it this morning and drop down the far side; otherwise R4 south past Morioka is the fast line. The last stretch into Naruko runs through the Naruko Gorge, which in mid-October is one of the best foliage runs in Tohoku.

Worth stopping for

  • Hachimantai Aspite Lineinfo — high, open, and near its foliage peak at this altitude.
  • Moriokainfo — Iwate Park, the Ishiwarizakura cherry tree growing out of a split granite boulder, and jajamen for lunch.
  • Hiraizumiinfo — the UNESCO temple complex; Chuson-ji's gold-leafed Konjikido is the one to see. Adds about an hour.
  • Geibikei Gorgeinfo — flat-bottomed boats poled through a limestone canyon, east of Hiraizumi.
  • Naruko Gorgeinfo — the Ofukazawa bridge viewpoint is the classic shot. Naruko town is also the home of kokeshi dolls, with workshops open to visitors.

Watch for

Naruko Gorge at peak colour on the Sunday of a long weekend will be busy. Ride it early or late, and expect the gorge car parks to be full in the middle of the day.

Lodging

Naruko Hotel

Rated 4.2 from nearly 1,700 reviews, operating since 1873 and five minutes from the station. Water is drawn from several sources and is often milky white — the colour shifts day to day. Private baths can be booked for about ¥2,000 for fifty minutes, the yukata are printed with kokeshi, and the dinner buffet gets consistently good marks. The whole town smells of sulphur.

www.narukohotel.co.jp ↗

Monday 10/12

Naruko → Kaminoyama

Naruko130 kmKaminoyama
Open this day's route in Google Maps ↗

On the road

The shortest riding day of the trip, and on a national holiday that is exactly right. R47 west along the Mogami valley, then south through Obanazawa to Yamagata and on to Kaminoyama. Gentle roads, and enough spare time to make two or three real stops rather than riding past everything.

Worth stopping for

  • Ginzan Onseninfo — a short detour north of Obanazawa; Taisho-era wooden inns facing each other across a stream, gas lamps at dusk. The most photographed hot spring street in Japan, and it earns it.
  • Yamadera, if you passed it by on Day 4info — it is on this route too.
  • Kaminoyama Castleinfo — and the town's foot baths, this time with an afternoon to use them.

Watch for

Holiday Monday is the national return day, so expressways toward Tokyo will be solid. You are travelling the other way on back roads — no problem, but do not be tempted onto anything heading south.

Lodging

Hatago-no-kokoro Hashimotoya

Rated 4.5 from 338 reviews — the highest-rated stop on the trip, and a few doors from where you stayed on Day 3. A rabbit motif runs through the whole building down to the tableware, meals are served in private rooms, and both public baths have open-air sections. Reviewers describe the layout as a small maze and the presentation as the reason to come.

www.hasimotoya.com ↗

Tuesday 10/13

Kaminoyama → Aizu Higashiyama

Kaminoyama230 kmAizu Higashiyama
Open this day's route in Google Maps ↗

On the road

South on R13 through Yonezawa, then over to Fukushima and up the Bandai-Azuma Skyline — a high volcanic road running along the Azuma range at around 1,600 m past bare sulphur slopes and dwarf pine. It drops to Inawashiro, from where you cut west to Aizuwakamatsu.

Worth stopping for

  • Bandai-Azuma Skylineinfo — stop at Jododaira for Azuma-Kofuji, an almost perfectly symmetrical crater you can walk up to the rim of in fifteen minutes.
  • Tsubakuro Valleyinfo — and the Fudosawa bridge above it, near the Fukushima end of the Skyline.
  • Goshiki-numainfo — the five coloured ponds on the Ura-Bandai side, each a different shade from mineral content. A flat 3.6 km trail links them.
  • Lake Inawashiroinfo — the fourth largest in Japan, with Mt. Bandai reflected in it on a still morning.
  • Tsuruga Castleinfo — in Aizuwakamatsu, ten minutes from the inn.

Watch for

The Skyline has a seasonal night closure and volcanic gas restrictions near Jododaira. If it is shut, R13 south to R121 through Kitakata is the fallback and cuts the day to about 160 km.

Lodging

Konjakutei

Rated 4.3 from 252 reviews, at Higashiyama Onsen on the edge of Aizuwakamatsu. A small property where the service and the food are what reviewers write about — several mention the kitchen accommodating dietary requests without fuss. The baths here are indoor and modest, but guests can walk 150 m to the sister ryokan Harataki for an open-air bath facing the waterfall.

konjakutei.yumeguri.co.jp/index.php ↗

Wednesday 10/14

Aizu Higashiyama → Yunishigawa

Aizu Higashiyama150 kmYunishigawa
Open this day's route in Google Maps ↗

On the road

South on R121, the old Aizu-Nishi highway, through thatched-roof country and river gorges to Kawaji, then east up a dead-end valley to Yunishigawa. The last stretch is narrow and quiet and ends where the road does.

Worth stopping for

  • Ouchi-jukuinfo — a preserved Edo post town of thatched houses on an unpaved street. Touristed, but genuinely striking, and the negi-soba eaten with a spring onion instead of chopsticks is a local ritual.
  • Tonohetsuriinfo — eroded cliff formations above the Okawa river, reached by a footbridge.
  • Yunokami Onsen stationinfo — the only thatched railway station in Japan, with a foot bath on the platform.
  • Yunishigawa villageinfo — founded by Heike refugees hiding after Dan-no-ura. Households here still avoid carp streamers and crowing roosters, on the grounds that either might have given the fugitives away.

Watch for

There is very little in the valley beyond the hotels, so dinner is wherever you are staying. Fill up in Kawaji rather than assuming you will find fuel further in.

Lodging

Kamenoi Hotel Nikko Yunishigawa

Rated 3.7 from 544 reviews, and the reviews split cleanly. It runs all-inclusive — drinks from 2pm, snacks from 4pm, late-night noodles — and reviewers consistently praise the food, the onsen and the setting. They just as consistently flag that the guest rooms are dated: this was the Heike Honjin before Kamenoi took it over, and the refurbishment reached the public areas more than the bedrooms.

kamenoi-hotels.com/nikkoyunishigawa ↗

Thursday 10/15

Yunishigawa → Tokyo

Yunishigawa220 kmTokyo
Open this day's route in Google Maps ↗

On the road

Back down the valley to Kawaji and Kinugawa, then the Nikko-Utsunomiya road onto the Tohoku Expressway and south into the city. Straightforward riding, but it ends with Tokyo traffic and a handover, so build in margin.

Worth stopping for

  • Kinugawa Onseninfo — worth a stop for the gorge if not the town.
  • Nikko Toshoguinfo — and the Shinkyo bridge, if you swing west before turning south. Adds about two hours.
  • Utsunomiya for gyozainfo — the city is famous for it and it is right on the route.

Watch for

Leave a buffer before the Rushcorp handover. City riding at the end of a long trip is where mistakes happen.

Lodging

Tokyo — final night

Nothing booked yet. Onyado Nono Asakusa is worth considering: tatami throughout, its own hot spring baths, free evening ramen, and two minutes from the airport limousine stop for the morning flight. Asakusa View Hotel is the other option — bigger rooms and the limousine bus at the door, though a number of reviewers report noise.

Friday 10/16

Depart

On the road

Airport limousine from Asakusa to Haneda, or the Tsukuba Express and Keikyu if you prefer rail.

Booking priority. Hachimantai Country Club for Sat 10/10 — it is an 18-hole course on the Sports Day long weekend, and reviewers report long waits. Then the two Hachimantai nights, then Naruko.

Two long days. 10/7, 10/8 and 10/13 all run around 230 km with a mountain road inside them. Everything else has slack, and 10/12 is deliberately short because it falls on the holiday.

Before you ride. Carry the international driving permit, home licence and passport together. Confirm seasonal status for R292, the Chōkai Blue Line and the Bandai-Azuma Skyline — all three have night closures or volcanic gas restrictions. Closure links are on the bikes page.
