The shared load

Lighter and quicker to flick into a corner than a loaded 1300, but down on power everywhere else. This is the bike that sets the group's pace.

Riding notes

  • Let the F750 lead. Jenifer will pull away two-up on expressways and long climbs. Riding to the slower bike keeps the solo rider from working hard all day.
  • The pillion sets the stopping rhythm. No engine heat, no wind management, nothing to do with their hands — a passenger stiffens and chills faster than either rider. Break every 60–90 minutes, not every fuel stop.

Fuel and cold

  • Fill before the mountains, not after. The Chōkai Blue Line, the Aspite Line, R292 over Shibu Pass and the Bandai-Azuma Skyline all have long stretches with nothing open.
  • Rural hours are the real limit, not range. Tōhoku stations often close by 18:00–19:00, some shut on Sundays, and unmanned pumps frequently reject foreign cards.
  • Dress for 2,172 m (7,126 ft). Shibu Pass in October is near freezing at the top with wind chill on it. Thermal layers, heated grips on.
  • Documents. Both riders carry the international driving permit — booklet form, Section A stamped for motorcycles — together with the home licence and passport. The pillion needs only a passport.

Check before every mountain day

Mountain roads in Japan close at short notice for snow, rockfall, volcanic gas and roadworks, and the notice usually appears only on the road manager's own site in Japanese. Check the night before, then again at breakfast — a gate closed at the foot of a pass can cost you the whole day. Use your phone's translate function on these.

  • JARTIC — nationwide live traffic and closures. The map view is the fastest way to see a closure on your route. jartic.or.jp ↗
  • Japan Road Traffic Information (mobile). Same data, simpler on a phone at the roadside. jartic.or.jp/service/mobile ↗
  • NEXCO East — expressway conditions. Covers the Kan-etsu and Tōhoku expressways you use on 10/5, 10/12 and 10/15. driveplaza.com/traffic ↗
  • MLIT Tōhoku Regional Bureau — national routes. The authority for R292, R7, R13 and R121. thr.mlit.go.jp/road ↗
  • Gunma Prefecture — R292 over Shibu Pass (10/6). Night closure plus volcanic gas restrictions near Shirane, decided day by day. pref.gunma.jp/site/douro ↗
  • Chōkai Blue Line (10/9) and Bandai-Azuma Skyline. Both are seasonal with night gates; the Skyline also closes for volcanic gas. Check via MLIT Tōhoku ↗

Faster than any of these

Ask the ryokan front desk the evening before. Innkeepers on these routes know the gate status better than any website, and Ninja Tours can confirm in Japanese if you would rather not rely on a translated page. Treat the links above as the backup, not the primary source.
